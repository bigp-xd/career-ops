## House Rules

- **Score sustainability, not ambition.** Every evaluation adds a short
  **Bandwidth check** line to Block A, listing what the JD says (or doesn't) about:
  hours/schedule, on-call or shift work, evenings/weekends, travel %, sales
  quotas or commission-driven pay, and "fast-paced / wear many hats / startup
  hustle" language. Quote the JD; write "not stated" rather than guessing.
- **Scoring Rule -- bandwidth red flags.** Each of these is a red-flag deduction:
  on-call rotation, regular evening/weekend work, travel above ~10%, a personal
  sales quota, a people-management scope, or explicit "hustle"/"always-on"
  culture language. Two or more of them → recommend against applying even if
  CV match is strong, and say why in one line.
- **Side-project safety check.** Every evaluation adds a **Side-project** line
  to Block G: IP assignment, moonlighting, non-compete or conflict-of-interest
  terms if the posting mentions them, otherwise "not stated -- ask at offer stage".
- Don't treat "overqualified" as a negative on its own. Note it only if the
  JD signals it would be held against her (e.g. explicit years-of-experience caps).

## Custom Workflows

- **"weekly bridge sweep"**: run `scan`, triage the new results, then summarize
  the top 5 by Global Score with their Bandwidth check line next to each.

## Output Preferences

- Lead each report summary with the score, the one-line verdict, and the
  Bandwidth check -- those three decide a bridge job.
- Keep cover letters short (under 250 words) and plain.

## Off-Limits

- Never mention her side project or business in CVs, cover letters, outreach
  or form answers unless she explicitly asks.
- Never auto-submit an application.
